Why HIFU is perfect for travelers

High-Intensity Focused Ultrasound (HIFU) is the non-surgical treatment most frequently requested by travelers to Tokyo — and for good reason. It requires zero downtime, can be completed in 60–90 minutes, and you can fly home the same day. There's no visible redness, no peeling, no bandages. You walk out of the clinic looking exactly as you walked in.

The results — gradual skin tightening and lifting — develop over the following 2–3 months as the ultrasound energy stimulates collagen production. This means your investment keeps paying dividends long after you've returned home.

What happens during a HIFU session

Consultation and assessment

Before your session, the physician will assess your skin laxity, discuss your goals, and determine the appropriate depth settings and number of shots. The depth (cartridge type) is selected based on the anatomy of each area — deeper cartridges target the SMAS layer (the same layer addressed in surgical facelifts); shallower ones address superficial skin tightening.

The treatment

A gel is applied to the skin. The physician moves the HIFU handpiece across the treatment area in a grid pattern, delivering focused ultrasound energy at precise depths. Most patients feel a warm, prickling sensation — described as mild to moderate discomfort, not sharp pain. Some areas (bony areas near temples, jawline) are more sensitive than others.

Immediately after

You may experience mild redness or warmth for 30–60 minutes post-treatment — this is normal and resolves quickly. There is no visible marking, swelling, or bruising in the vast majority of cases. You can apply makeup and continue your day normally. No restrictions on flying.

Who is the ideal HIFU candidate?

Best results for:

Ages 30–60Mild–Moderate LaxityPreventive CarePre-Event Prep

HIFU works best for patients with mild to moderate skin laxity — often people in their 30s, 40s, and 50s seeing early sagging, jowl formation, or loss of jawline definition. It is also excellent for people in their late 20s–30s as a preventive treatment to delay visible aging.

Results are more subtle than surgical lifting and develop gradually. Patients with severe sagging may not achieve their desired result from HIFU alone and should consider consulting about surgical options.

Not ideal for: Very thin skin with minimal fat layer, patients with active skin infections, recent facial surgery within 6 months, or those with pacemakers or certain implants in the treatment area.

HIFU results: realistic expectations

HIFU results are real but gradual. Most patients see initial improvement within 2–4 weeks as inflammation subsides and early collagen remodeling begins. The full result appears at approximately 3 months and continues to develop slightly through the 6-month mark.

One session per year is typically sufficient for maintenance. The result isn't dramatic in the way that surgery is — it's a refreshed, lifted appearance that colleagues and friends notice without being able to identify the cause. This natural-looking outcome is precisely why Tokyo's conservative aesthetic philosophy suits HIFU so well.

Common questions from travelers

Can I really fly home the same day?

Yes. HIFU has no flying restrictions. The only post-treatment recommendation is to avoid intense sun exposure on the treated area for a few days and apply SPF when outdoors.

How long until I see results?

Initial improvement is visible within 2–4 weeks. Full results develop over 3 months. You won't see an immediate dramatic change on the day of treatment — which is actually reassuring, since it means results look natural rather than "done."

Is it painful?

Discomfort varies by individual and treatment area. Most patients describe a warm, prickling sensation — noticeable but tolerable. Some clinics offer a mild numbing gel or oral pain relief for sensitive patients. The treatment is never described as severe pain by the vast majority of patients.

How does HIFU compare to a surgical facelift?

A surgical facelift produces more dramatic results with longer-lasting lift (5–10 years). HIFU is non-invasive, requires zero downtime, and costs significantly less, but the lifting effect is subtler and lasts 12–18 months per session. They address different degrees of laxity and suit different patient profiles.

Which HIFU device is best?

Ultherapy is the most studied and FDA-cleared HIFU device. Other devices — including various Korean brands — can produce comparable results. What matters most is the physician's skill in selecting appropriate depths and delivering adequate energy, not the brand name alone.

Can I combine HIFU with other treatments on the same trip?

HIFU can be combined with Botox on the same day (Botox first, HIFU second) or on different days. Allow at least 2 weeks between HIFU and filler injections to avoid disrupting newly placed filler. Discuss your full treatment plan at the consultation.
